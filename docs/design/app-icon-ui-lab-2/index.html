App icon — the app's own grammar

Two rules from the product, not from taste: a branch leaves a block to the right or down and its arrow enters the child from the left — never upward — and the glyph is made of blocks and trunks, not of a page with text lines, because a page with lines is every document app.

1 · Four shapes

G · trunk

Parent, trunk, two children

The layout's own picture: a block on top, a trunk down its bottom edge, branches that go right and arrows that enter each child from the left. A tree seen from the side, drawn with the app's grammar.

At 16 px: three light blocks hanging off one blue stem. The stem is vertical, so it survives shrinking.

2 · Same shape, three colours

The glyph is decided; the colour is not. The accent is a user setting, so the icon cannot follow it — but it can take the app's own secondary tone.

Blue (the default accent) · the app's soft green · paper-grey, the quiet one.

3 · Why round 1 failed

Arrows went up. In the product a branch leaves to the right or goes down, and the head always enters the child from the left. An upward arrow is "growth", which is not what the app does — it is a tree that grows sideways and downwards.

The page with lines is generic. Squiggles mean "document"; every note app already owns that shape. The mark here must be blocks and trunks, with the text removed.

Lab only, no application code. The chosen SVG exports at 1024 with the heavier small-size variants, then iconset, then .icns.
