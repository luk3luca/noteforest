Geometric

No object, no tree. This is the sheet that bets on pure form: a letter that is a mark, a single stroke, a hole, a lattice, a cut. It is also where an app icon can look like a tool rather than like a stationery cupboard. The monograms are the only place where letters are allowed, because there the letter is the mark.

01

Monograms

G01–G08

N for NoteForest, drawn as geometry rather than as type. These are the only letters in the set, and they are the letters because the mark is the letter — not a caption.

02

Single strokes

G09–G16

One line does the whole icon. The family is a test of nerve: at 16 px it is a single bar and has to be enough. G09 to G16 are ordered from one stroke to three.

03

Negative space

G17–G24

A filled square is the cheapest shape in the world; a square with something removed is a mark. Every icon in this family is one paper field and one cut, and the cut is the identity.

04

Grids and dots

G25–G33

The note forest as a lattice. Dots are the only element that can be repeated nine or sixteen times and still read at 16 px, because the eye sees a texture rather than a count.

05

Concentric

G34–G40

Nesting is the cheapest way to make a shape feel deep without a gradient. Squares, circles and arcs, all hollow, all with a deliberate break somewhere.

06

Cuts

G41–G46

Paper folded as geometry. Every icon here is a division of one square into two or three regions, with no object left inside — the composition is the whole mark.

07

One line

G47–G50

A single continuous stroke, bent two or three times. The most minimal family in the whole set, and the one with the highest ratio of failures: a line is only a mark if its path is memorable.
