Sheet 1 of 5 · O01–O50

Object

Paper as a physical thing: sheets, stacks, folds, tickets, tags, notebooks, envelopes, paperweights. The bet here is that one object with a strong silhouette can carry the whole product without drawing the product. Sheet 5 tests the opposite bet — blocks and branches as the mark — and the two sheets are meant to be read against each other. Every tile is a real object at a real weight; the differences are cut, proportion, offset and fill, never a new decoration.

01

Sheets and stacks

O01–O10

The plainest family, and the one that has to carry the weakest idea. Silhouette varies by count, proportion and offset: one sheet, three, five, a tall strip, a square card, a landscape ledger.

02

Folds and cuts

O11–O18

One gesture per icon, applied to the same sheet. A fold changes the silhouette and the shading; a cut changes the silhouette alone. These are the cheapest distinct marks in the whole set.

03

Bookmarks and tickets

O19–O26

Silhouettes that are not rectangles: notches, points, tabs, holes. These are the shapes that survive at 16 px because the outline itself is unusual.

04

Notebooks and spines

O27–O34

Binding is a second object attached to the first: rings, stitches, a spine, a band. The left edge becomes the distinguishing feature, which is exactly where a Dock tile has the least room.

05

Weights and containers

O35–O42

Objects that hold or weigh other objects. This family is where a second material enters — metal, glass, board — and where the composition stops being a single centred rectangle.

06

Edges and negative space

O43–O50

The sheet reduced to what is left of it: a stack seen edge-on, a bite out of the top, a split in two, an outline with nothing inside. Several of these are deliberately compromised at 16 px.
