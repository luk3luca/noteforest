250 icon proposals, and the ten that survive a second look

Five contact sheets of fifty icons each, all drawn on the same geometry and shown at 96 px and at 16 px. This is a search space, not a shortlist: most of the 250 are mediocre, and a fair number are here only to mark where a family stops working. The point was to move fast across genuinely different silhouettes and then pick the few that still mean something at Dock size.

Everything is hand-written SVG in a single HTML file per sheet. No dependencies, no build step, no external assets, no network, no filters, no baked text.

Correction applied

Structure is in, and it has its own sheet. The first brief banned blocks-and-branches as too diagrammatic. That was wrong: the tree of blocks is the product's one distinctive idea, and an icon that avoids it is an icon of a generic notes app. Sheet 5 explores it as mass, under three hard rules — at most four shapes, edges at least a third of a block's weight, and a silhouette that reads at 16 px.

The earlier "no page with text lines" rule is also relaxed. The real problem was margins and emptiness, which the inset rule already fixes; pages with lines now appear at the correct scale in sheet 1.

Geometry, once

Every icon uses the same three boxes. The 100 px ring is air; the artwork lives between 64 and 120 px inside the squircle, so all four sides keep a visible border without the object floating in the dark.

  • Canvas 1024 × 1024
    0 0 1024 1024 — everything is drawn here, at any display size.
  • macOS squircle 824 × 824 at (100, 100)
    corner radius 185. The 100 px ring is air and is never drawn in.
  • Art box, inset 64
    164 … 860. The largest an object may be; used by the bars and the bleeding grove.
  • Art box, inset 120
    220 … 804. The quietest; used by single sheets and small discs.
  • Paper
    #f4f3ee with #fdfefd highlights and #c9c8c1 shade. The accent is #8fb0cd, the secondary tone #8fbf9f. The sample above sits exactly on the 120 px inset, the smallest an object may be.

The five sheets

Each sheet is one hypothesis about what the mark should be. They are meant to be read against each other: the winner is unlikely to be the best drawing in one sheet, and more likely to be the best idea across two.

SHEET 1 · O01–O50Object Paper as a physical thing: sheets, stacks, folds, tickets, tags, notebooks, envelopes, weights. The bet: one object with a real silhouette can carry the product. SHEET 2 · N01–N50Nature Leaves, canopy, trunks, groves, seeds, roots, rings, bark. The name says forest, so the sheet has to answer it — abstracted until it stops being a drawing of a tree. SHEET 3 · G01–G50Geometric Monograms, single strokes, negative space, lattices, concentric shapes, cuts, one-line marks. No object and no tree: pure form, and the only place where letters are allowed. SHEET 4 · M01–M50Material and strange Translucency, embossed paper, glass, duotone, inverted light, near-black, one shape, deliberate detail. The extremes are here to find the boundaries, and several are expected to fail. SHEET 5 · S01–S50Structure Blocks and branches as icon material, not as a screenshot of the UI. Four shapes maximum per icon, fat edges, no head where a head would smear, nothing pointing up.

How to read a tile

Every tile is the same drawing at two sizes: 96 px on the left, 16 px beside it. Nothing is scaled by the browser — both come from viewBox="0 0 1024 1024" with a different width, and the two variants are written separately, so the small one can drop a vein, a stitch, an arrowhead or a fold that would turn into grey mush. The small drawing may lose detail; it never changes the silhouette. The id above each name (O01 … S50) is stable and is what the shortlist refers to.

The shortlist

Ten out of 250, weighted toward structure because that is where the product's identity is. Each entry has two lines: what it is, and why it might hold — including the risk, because the risk is the useful part.

S08

Notch

One shape: a block with a branch-shaped cut in its right edge. It is the only icon that says "a branch leaves here" without drawing the branch.

At 16 the notch is still two pixels deep, so it survives — the risk is that it reads as a battery or a C before it reads as a note.

Sheet 5 · Structure

S09

Stub

Also one shape: a note whose branch leaves and goes nowhere yet. An open thread, which is what most notes actually are.

The most minimal mark in the set that still has a direction; the risk is that a lone arm can read as a key or a switch.

Sheet 5 · Structure

S03

One of each

The whole grammar in four shapes: one child to the right, one below, and a parent that carries its own prong so the budget holds.

It is the most literal possible statement of the product and it still reads at 16; the risk is that four masses become a blob in a Dock at 32 px.

Sheet 5 · Structure

S41

L

Two shapes and the letter is free: a tall parent, an elbow, a wide child underneath. The structure drawn as one continuous mass.

It fills the square better than any other structure icon and never turns to mush; the risk is that an L is also a corner bracket, which says nothing.

Sheet 5 · Structure

S48

Shared child

Two parents, one child: the DAG, with the second parent in grey as a reference edge. No other notes app can draw this shape.

The grey parent is what makes it legible at 16, and also what makes it fragile — a grey on a dark Dock is the first thing to disappear.

Sheet 5 · Structure

G02

N, cut

The letter cut out of a paper square: a letterpress block where the ink is the field and the paper is the plate.

The strongest monogram and one of the few that is unmistakable at 16; the risk is that an N in a square is a very common app-icon pattern.

Sheet 3 · Geometric

G43

Chevron

A solid chevron pointing right: the direction of every branch in the product, with mass instead of a stroke and no arrowhead to smear.

The most legible mark in the whole set at 16 px; the risk is that it is also the most generic — it could belong to a video player.

Sheet 3 · Geometric

M29

Ink window

A dark card with a paper window, on a paper field. The material inverted, and a hole where the content would be.

It is the only inverted icon that survives every size, because a hole reads at any resolution; the risk is that it reads as a socket or a stop sign.

Sheet 4 · Material

O19

Ribbon

One accent band with a notched foot — the only icon in the set made of a single accent silhouette and no paper at all.

It is the safest mark on this list and the most generic: a bookmark is every reading app's icon, which is exactly why it is here as the control.

Sheet 1 · Object

N08

Half leaf

Only the left half of a leaf exists: a blade with one straight edge. It is the one shape in the Nature sheet that stops being a leaf.

It is also the riskiest of the ten — a sliver with a straight side can read as a broken shape, which is either the idea or the failure.

Sheet 2 · Nature

What is deliberately left out

  • Arrows pointing up, and branches entering a block from below or from the right. Not a taste call: in this product a branch leaves right or down and enters the child on its left. Sheet 5 is drawn under that rule.
  • Interface screenshots as icons. Blocks connected by hairlines with arrowheads were the failure of the earlier rounds and are still absent; structure appears only when it is a mass with a silhouette.
  • Freeform canvas, pins, arbitrary drag. The layout is a fixed logical tree with adaptive geometry; nothing in the set suggests a whiteboard.
  • 3D clay / glossy Big Sur renders, gradients as ornament, drop shadows beyond a single flat offset. Wrong material for a calm, flat, paper-on-ink product.
  • The generic app-icon vocabulary: magnifiers, sparkles, gears, clouds, speech bubbles, pencils, checkmarks, interlocking rings. They would make the app unidentifiable in a Dock.
  • Text, letters as labels, emoji, external images, non-system fonts. The only letters are the monograms in sheet 3 and the NF ligature, where the letter is the mark itself.
  • iPad / Apple Pencil motifs. A later phase at most; nothing in v1 should promise it.
  • A single winner. The set is evidence, not a decision. The next step is to redraw the ten shortlisted shapes as a real iconset and look at them in the Dock, at 16 px, on light and dark desktops.
