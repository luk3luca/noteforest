Nature

The name says forest, so this sheet has to answer it — but abstracted until it stops being a drawing of a tree. The rule inside the sheet: one gesture, no ground lines, no branch drawing, no cute. A leaf is a closed shape with a fold, a canopy is a mass with one bite out of it, a trunk is a taper. Where a shape could come from a stock pack, it is in the sheet on purpose, as the control.

01

Leaves

N01–N10

The most dangerous family: a leaf is one step from clip art. The variants are cut, fold, half, outline, and proportion. N08 is the one that stops being a leaf.

02

Canopy

N11–N20

A tree seen as a mass, not as a symbol. The silhouette varies by the canopy's shape — dome, bar, stepped, half-disc, blob, crescent, ring — while the trunk stays one vertical, because the trunk is the page.

03

Trunks

N21–N28

No canopy at all. The trunk alone is a taper, a fork, an elbow, a slot. This is the family that survives 16 px best, because a thick vertical is the easiest shape to see at one pixel.

04

Groves

N29–N36

Three or four bars are the whole idea of a forest, and the family that failed once before as "three vertical bars". The difference here is height rhythm, tone and cropping — the bars are not equal, and the field between them is part of the drawing.

05

Seeds and roots

N37–N44

Where a forest starts and where it holds. Seeds are single closed shapes; roots are the only family allowed to be intricate, because a root system is legible as a texture even when the branches are not.

06

Rings and bark

N45–N50

Wood as a material and time as a pattern. Rings are the one place where circles belong on this sheet; bark is the only place where a texture is allowed to be a texture.
