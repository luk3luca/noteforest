Material and strange

Not a shape but a surface: translucent paper, embossed card, glass, duotone, ink on a paper field, near-black on black. Half of this sheet is deliberately extreme — one shape with nothing else, or so much detail that it dies at 16 px. The extremes are here to find the boundaries, and several tiles are expected to fail.

01

Translucency

M01–M08

Layers that show each other. Only opacity, no blur and no gradient: on the dark field a translucent sheet is a blue-grey pane, and the overlap becomes the brightest part of the icon.

02

Embossed paper

M09–M16

Depth from two lit edges, never from a filter. One light from the top-left, one shadow from the bottom-right, and the material appears. This is the most likely family to survive a "no gradients" rule.

03

Glass and duotone

M17–M24

Two colours and one surface. Duotone is the safest thing in this sheet; glass is the riskiest, because a translucent pane over a dark field is almost nothing.

04

Inverted light

M25–M32

Paper field, ink artwork. In a Dock full of dark squares these are the icons that are found without looking — and the ones that look wrong on a dark desktop. Both facts are useful.

05

Near-black

M33–M38

The dark end of the range, deliberately pushed too far. These are the tiles that answer the question "how dark can the icon be and still exist in a dark Dock". Most of them fail, which is the finding.

06

One shape

M39–M44

The floor of the exercise: a single geometric figure, no second idea. If one of these works, it works because of scale and proportion alone — and at 16 px it is indistinguishable from a system icon.

07

Deliberately intricate

M45–M50

Too much detail, on purpose. Every icon here will lose most of its information at 16 px; the question is whether the silhouette that remains is still worth shipping. Predictions are in the captions.
