The page, filling the frame

One object, near the edges: the margin is the squircle, not the drawing. Four ways for a note to own the whole icon, each distinguished by a single fold, cut or spine — no arrows, no connectors, nothing to decode.

T1 · the fold

Page with a folded corner

The sheet owns the icon and the fold owns the identity: the only coloured surface, at the top right where the corner is rounded anyway.

At 16 px: a white tile with a cut corner. The fold changes the silhouette, so it survives when the lines are gone.

T2 · the stack

Page with a second page behind

Two notes, not one: the back sheet is a hard edge at the right and bottom, so the stack reads as depth rather than as decoration.

At 16 px: a white tile with a grey lip on two sides. The lip is what makes it "many notes".

T3 · the spine

Page with a bound edge

A notebook seen edge-on: a dark spine on the left with a hairline of accent. The paper fills everything else, so nothing is empty.

At 16 px: a light tile with a dark band and a blue thread. Reads as a bound note instantly.

T4 · the bite

Page with a corner taken out

A quarter-circle of the field cut out of the bottom-right corner, with a hairline of accent on the cut. The silhouette carries the mark; the paper still fills the rest.

At 16 px: a light tile with a bite. The strongest silhouette of the four and the most "designed".

In the Dock

The fix. Paper now covers the icon edge to edge, clipped by the squircle — the margin is the shape itself. The lines are thick enough to read at 32 and are dropped entirely at 16, where only the silhouette and the accent detail survive.

My pick: T1. The fold is the smallest possible gesture that changes the silhouette, and the paper is the app's own surface. T3 is the most "study" of the four; T4 the most graphic; T2 the most literal.
