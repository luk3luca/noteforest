App icon — the I family

The I structure kept: one block beside its parent, one below it through the trunk, arrows entering from the left. The lines are back — but only inside the parent, so the glyph reads as a note that branches instead of as a page of text.

1 · Four ways to draw the same idea

I1 · recommended

Note, right child, below child

Three shapes and two connectors: the note with its lines, the sibling beside it, the child that wrapped below through the trunk. The packing rule, readable.

At 16 px the arrows go: what stays is a lined block, a block to its right and one under it. That is still the idea.

I2 · tighter

Children flush right

The same three blocks, but the children share one right edge and the trunk runs longer. More orderly, less organic.

At 16 px: a clear left column and a clear right column — the strongest silhouette of the four.

I3 · two shapes

Note and one child below

Drop the right child: one note, one branch that leaves the bottom and turns right. The cleanest at small sizes.

At 16 px: a lined block and a block under it, joined from below. Nothing to lose.

I4 · outline

Note and two outline rows

The children become thin rows: the note plus its outline. The most "this is a note app" and the least "tree".

At 16 px: a lined block with two small bars to the right — reads as a list, not as a structure.

3 · What the small sizes keep

16 px holds two blocks and one connector at most. The 16 variants in this lab already drop the arrowheads and thicken the strokes; below that, the lines inside the note become one bar, not three.

Where to iterate: I1 is the only one that tells both halves of the story (a sibling to the right, a child below). If it feels crowded, the next step is not a new shape — it is spacing: widen the gap between the note and its children and shorten the trunk.

Lab only, no application code. Chosen SVG → 1024 + small-size variants → iconset → .icns.
