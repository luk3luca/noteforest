App icon — four roads

Connectors now leave the card 12 px outside its edge and the head touches the child without entering it. Twelve proposals in four families: the fixed structural one, two letterforms that are also the app's grammar, abstract marks, and four deliberately strange ones.

A · Structure, with the connector fixed

The I family from round 3, minus the overlap. Same story: a note, a sibling beside it, a child below.

A1

Note, sibling, child below

The fixed I: a 12 px gap at each departure, the head stops on the child's edge.

At 16 px: a lined block, a block to its right, one below.

A2 · green

The same, in the forest tone

Identical structure with the app's soft green. It is the only green the interface already uses, so it does not invent a colour.

At 16 px: same silhouette; the green reads a touch softer than blue on a dark Dock.

B · Letterforms that are the grammar

If the mark has to be read at a glance, a letter is the strongest at 16 px — provided the letter is drawn with the app's own strokes.

B1 · the letter F

F for Forest

A trunk and a top branch make the F; the lower branch is the app's arrow with a block at its end. The letter and the grammar are the same drawing.

At 16 px: an F. Unmistakable, and it carries the name.

64 32 16

B2 · the letter N

N as two trunks and a bridge

Two verticals are two trunks; the diagonal is the branch that joins them. It also reads as the initial.

At 16 px: a clear N. The diagonal is the only "up" stroke in the whole set — a letter, not an arrow.

B3 · abstract E

Trunk, three arms, one arrow

The purest structure: a trunk with three horizontal arms, the last one an arrow. Less letter, more diagram.

At 16 px: a stem with bars — reads as "outline" more than "tree".

C1 · abstract

Dot, branch, two dots

Nothing but nodes and connectors: no paper, no card, no letter. The most "system" and the most neutral.

At 16 px: three dots and a fork. Clean, but it could be any graph tool.

C · Abstract, no paper at all

Marks made of geometry only. They win at small sizes and lose the "note" half of the name.

C2 · the bracket

Corner and a block

A heavy corner is the parent's two edges; the block inside is the note. Two elements, maximum legibility.

At 16 px: a square in a corner. Bold and quiet at once.

C3 · interlocking

Two nested branches

Two Ls, one paper one accent, nested: the branch inside the branch. Abstract, no repetition of shapes.

At 16 px: two corners, one blue. Distinctive, but it stops saying "note".

64 32 16

C4 · cut

Frame with a cut

A block outline cut by one horizontal branch: the sheet and the seam, as a single gesture. The most "system" of the four.

At 16 px: a square cut in half. Extremely legible, and slightly cold.

64 32 16

D1 · extravagant

A deck of notes

Three offset sheets, slightly tilted, and one branch leaving the pile. It says "many notes" instead of one.

At 16 px: a light mass with a blue tail — the tilt becomes noise. This is why it stays a lab.

D · Strange on purpose

Proposals that break the rules to see whether one of them points somewhere. A light icon in a dark Dock, a shape that is only negative space, a single inverted stroke.

D2 · inverted

Paper icon, ink branch

The icon is paper, the branch is ink. In a dark Dock a light square is immediately findable, and the paper tone is the app's own surface.

At 16 px: a bright tile with a dark blue corner — the most visible of the whole set.

64 32 16

D3 · one weight

Monoline trunk and dot

One stroke weight for everything, one dot as the only filled shape. Almost a pictogram, very calm.

At 16 px: a bracket and a dot. Distinct, though it reads more "settings" than "notes".

64 32 16

D4 · the odd one

Diamond note

The note is a diamond, the branch cuts into it. No paper shape, no rectangle — and it is unmistakable in a Dock.

At 16 px: a bright diamond. Memorable, but it says nothing about notes or trees. Included as a boundary test.

E · In the Dock, the four most distinct

A1 · B1 · C2 · D2. The fake icon on the left is only a neighbour.

Fixed in this round: every connector starts 12 px outside the card, so the round cap no longer eats into the paper; the arrowhead stops on the child's edge.

Where I would go next: B1 is the only one that carries the name and the structure at once. If the letter feels too explicit, A1 is the honest fallback; if the icon has to be recognisable in a crowded Dock, D2 (light paper) wins on sight.

Lab only, no application code. Chosen SVG → 1024 + small-size variants → iconset → .icns.
