NoteForest — from the name, not from the diagram

Everything before this was the layout drawn small: blocks, connectors, arrows. That is a screenshot, not a mark. This round starts from the two words — note and forest — and from the app's material: paper on a dark field, calm, no decoration.

R1 · the grove

Three pages standing

A forest is vertical, and so is a page. Three paper columns of different heights: a grove seen from the front, made of the app's own surface. No arrows, nothing to decode.

At 16 px: three light bars. Reads instantly, and it is the only shape in every round that could not belong to another app.

R2 · the note

One page, one shadow

The most honest object: a single sheet of paper with its own weight, and a second sheet behind it. Material honesty instead of a diagram.

At 16 px: a white card. Perfectly legible, and perfectly generic — every notes app has this icon.

R3 · the fold

Page with a folded corner

One gesture, one meaning: paper that has been handled. The folded corner is the only coloured surface, and it reads as a leaf.

At 16 px: a page with a cut corner. Quietly specific, and it survives because the fold is a silhouette change.

R4 · inverted grove

Paper field, dark grove

The same forest, inverted: a paper square with the trees cut out in ink. In a Dock full of dark squares it is the one that is found without looking.

At 16 px: three dark bars on white. The boldest of the four, and the least "dark mode app".

In the Dock

What changed. No connectors, no arrows, no blocks-as-UI. One metaphor per icon, at most three shapes, and real material: a dark field with a soft top light, paper with a shadow, one accent surface.

My pick: R1. It is the only one where "note" and "forest" live in the same gesture — a grove of pages — and the only one that a screenshot at 16 px cannot confuse with another app. R3 is the sophisticated fallback; R4 is the loud one; R2 is beautiful but generic.
